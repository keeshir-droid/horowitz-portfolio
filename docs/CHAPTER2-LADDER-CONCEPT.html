Part 2 · the whole chapter in one picture

The route

Scrolling moves you along this path from left to right. Each ladder takes you higher, so the climb itself shows you levelling up. Each floor runs event → photo → next ladder. The camera follows you closely; this zoomed-out view is only for explaining.

Part 3 · frame by frame

What someone scrolling sees

1 · Select screen. Chapter 1 pixelates away and the arcade boots up. Only one fighter is unlocked, and it's you. The other slots are locked. The cursor picks you.

2 · Ladder 1. You climb. The floor above glows, and the word KARATE peeks over the edge.

3 · The fight. The top floor turns into the arena and the Tekken HUD slams in. You wear your gi for the whole karate floor (climb, fight, photo). The opponent stays a faceless shadow, because the point is the tournament, not a person. It ends in a K.O.

4 · Walking on: the photo. You walk past a big framed photo on the wall: you with the medal. Every photo gets the same frame and colour grade, so it fits the art.

5 · Ladder 2: the stage. The curtains open, a spotlight lands on you, you take a bow, and the audience counter runs up to 2,000. About 2 seconds. Then you walk past the stage photo.

6 · Ladder 3: student council. You're giving a speech at the podium. About 2 seconds. Six badges, Grade 5 to Grade 10, light up one by one, so "every year" can be read at a glance. Then the council photo.

7 · The end. At the top, the select screen comes back, and a second slot unlocks: the high-school you. That's the door into Chapter 3. Every chapter can open this way, with the same screen in a new art style.

Timing · the whole chapter is short

About 15 seconds of normal scrolling: select screen 1s · climb 1s · karate fight 6s · medal photo 1s · stage bow 2s · photo 1s · council speech 2s · photo 1s · unlock 1s. Karate is the only long moment.

Why this fixes the "looks AI" problem

  • A plain caption bar on every frame. The game is the feel; the caption is the fact. Someone skimming reads only the captions and still gets everything.
  • No invented text. No fake stats or made-up fighters. The only game element is the karate fight, which was a real fight.
  • Real photos, but in frames on a wall, never in the menu, so the art direction stays clean.
