Chapter 1 · three options to pick from

A, B or C

These are quick concept frames, not the real build. They show roughly how each option would look and feel so you can choose. The final art will be tidier. In every option: colour, a Pokémon Red-style text box for each win, and a clear break into Chapter 2.

All art here is original. The look is “Game Boy Color-era top-down RPG”, not copied from any game. The kid is shown as short hair, round glasses, brown skin, a black jacket, a white tee, jeans and white sneakers. No uniform.

Option A

Recolour the side view CHEAPEST

Keep the current side-scrolling street. Add colour, the text boxes and a Chapter 2 title card.

FEELA cosy diorama. You walk left to right past things. Closest to what's already built, so it feels the least “Pokémon”.
COSTAbout 20–30 min. Roughly a quarter of the last Chapter 1 cycle. Reuses the existing kid, props and shelf with a new palette.
TRADE-OFFNot top-down. You said you'd like top-down if it works well, and A can't give you that.

Option B · recommended

Lean top-down town BEST VALUE

One compact Pokémon-style town seen from above. The kid walks along a path between four spots as you scroll.

FEELImmediately reads as “a Pokémon town”. You follow a little loop path: chess board, easel, science kit, robot, then the shelf window.
COSTAbout 45–60 min. Roughly the last cycle. New tiles and a 3-direction kid (down, up, side).
TRADE-OFFThe world is small, about two screens. It's a charming vignette, not an open map, and that's right for a 15-second chapter.

Option C

Full scrolling top-down world MOST COSTLY

A longer map the camera follows (the dashed box is what the viewer sees at once): a school, a stream, fences, other kids.

FEELRichest and most explorable. More life: NPC kids, a stream with a bridge, a school building.
COSTAbout 90 min or more. Needs a real tile map, camera follow, NPCs and much more art.
TRADE-OFFToo much for 15 seconds and for a chapter a16z cares least about. I'd avoid it.
