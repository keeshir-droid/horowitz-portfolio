## Case study: Solse (https://trysolse.com)

Software that learns your brand. Two products for product brands: Mona makes ads, and Luce finds and tests the next product before you make it.

Role: Founder: product, architecture, design, build, 2026. Page: https://risheeknawka.com/#/solse

### The problem

- AI ad makers produce generic ads.
- They distort the real product: labels smudge and shapes drift.
- They garble the words on the ad.
- They forget every correction, so editing is frustrating and nothing stays on-brand.
- The result looks like AI, and shoppers don’t trust it.
- Testing a new product or category is slow and costly: time, inventory, R&D and an uncertain result.

### What I built

I designed Solse’s architecture, the product and the ad engine: brand parameters, ads made from the brand’s real product photo, text added by code so it’s never garbled, and checks before and after each image. I built it with AI coding tools, launched it at trysolse.com, and brands have already joined the waitlist.

LOUD, Drowsy, Zest. and nuit. are made-up brands, and every sample image here is AI-generated.

### The bet

Generic ads come from generic context. The moat is depth of personalization: a brand profile that is specific, traceable and keeps learning. Features are not the moat. One engine serves every brand, so a new customer is a new config, not new code.

### Architecture

- **Monorepo**: TypeScript, npm workspaces. apps/web: the marketing site (Next.js 14), live. apps/mona: the ad engine and app. apps/luce: the testing engine (not started). packages/core: the shared brand-config types.
- **Infrastructure**: Vercel, auto-deploys every push to main. Supabase (Postgres) stores the waitlist, referrals and contact messages. Vercel Web Analytics: cookieless, query strings stripped.
- **Models**: Images: nano-banana, an image-edit model, through fal.ai. Copy and image QA: Gemini, strict JSON output. No API key? Every model step falls back to a fixed template, so the app still runs end to end.

### The brand profile

Each brand is one typed config, the “brand pack”. It covers allowed backgrounds, light and cast; how each product type behaves physically; text style, copy rules, claims and banned words; and checks. Every value is stored with its reason and its source, so every decision the engine makes can be traced back to who set it and why. (Why<T>: value, why, source.) Designed next: each parameter carries a confidence level (said, then seen, then confirmed). A customer’s word only becomes an enforced rule once it is grounded: measured by code, picked from a fixed list, anchored to their own approved and rejected examples, or backed by a product fact.

### The Mona pipeline (built)

1. **Real product in.** The model edits a scene around the brand’s own product photo instead of redrawing the product.
2. **Prompt compiler.** Builds each image prompt from the brand pack, and tags every prompt line with the field it came from. Describe what things are, never “no X”: image models ignore negatives. Fixed lists for background, shot, layout and action, so a batch really varies. Never put the brand name, or any text, in the prompt.
3. **Lint before spending.** Blocks an ad before any money is spent if it uses a banned background, a pose models draw wrongly, or a brief that contradicts itself.
4. **Spend gate.** One module is the only one that spends money. It shows a cost estimate, needs a human to confirm, and makes one test image before the batch. Jobs run in parallel with a concurrency cap.
5. **Text added by code, never drawn by the model.** Copy is checked against the brand’s claims and banned words, then drawn by code. Text colour comes from the measured brightness behind it. A soft band is added only where the background is busy. A new headline costs nothing, because the image isn’t regenerated.
6. **Checks after the image.** Both are advisory, with a redo button; nothing respends on its own. A pixel check measures how much of the background is plain white or grey. A vision-model realism check scores 1–5 and flags hands, faces, scale, fused cloth and a product that looks pasted on.

The pixel-check threshold came from 24 real kept and rejected ads. Rejected ads were 46–99% of the background plain white or grey; kept ads 0–9%.

### Designed, not built yet

- **The learning loop**: On a “no”, Mona asks why, and the answer becomes a proposed rule change the brand approves. Small taste changes apply automatically and can be undone. Claims, spend and boundaries always need a click.
- **Two layers**: A shared craft layer (realism and product physics) improves for every brand. A private taste layer belongs to each brand and is never used for another.
- **Three scores, never blended**: Fidelity: is it really their product? Fit: is it how they’d show it? Realism: does it pass as a real photo?
- **Variety**: Repeat the codes (logo, colours, tone), never the content (scene, hook, angle). Variety is checked against the batch, the brand’s history and the category.

### Luce, the testing engine (designed)

Luce finds and tests the next product before a brand makes it.

1. Mine Reddit, X, forums and reviews for demand.
2. Shape the product concepts: a look, a promise and a price.
3. Test them with Meta ads, keeping the creative constant.
4. Report the ad’s pull (clicks) and the product’s pull (paid deposits) separately, against the brand’s own metric.

Luce never makes the business call: no “sell / don’t sell”, no inventory amounts, and nothing runs without the brand’s consent.

### Design decisions

- **Type is never drawn by the image model.** That fixed garbled text and made editing free.
- **Lint before spending.** Bad ads are caught before they cost money, not after.
- **A human confirms every spend.** And one image is made before a batch.
- **Every rule shows why.** Why it exists and where it came from. No black-box brand kit.
- **Luce’s boundary.** It suggests and tests; the founder decides.
- **The look.** Premium, product-led SaaS: white, the Switzer typeface, real UI mocks and real sample images.
